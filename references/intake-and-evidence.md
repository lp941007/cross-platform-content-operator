# Intake and Evidence

## Minimum working brief

Accept any available subset: product/service, proof or selling points, rough idea, platforms, objective, people, locations, budget, and schedule. Do not require a complete brief merely to start.

## Evidence labels

| Label | Use for | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed | User directly states a fact | Use with clear attribution |
| Verified | Reliable source checked now | Cite source and date |
| Inference | Reasonable analysis from known facts | Mark as inference |
| Unresolved | Information is missing or contradictory | Ask or state a conservative assumption |
| Unsupported | Cannot be evidenced or creates an unjustified promise | Do not use |

## Missing-information rule

Ask one question, prioritized in this order: prohibited claim/compliance, audience and objective, proof, conversion action, budget/production constraint, then preference. If a low-risk gap remains, continue with a stated assumption and name the condition that would replace it.

Never infer a product capability from a category name. Example: “premium” is a positioning claim, not proof of material, design, safety, or price.

When only a category or product name is known, do not write performance, safety, material, price, or comparative selling points. You may deliver a required-evidence list, a filming verification plan, and non-claim-based scene directions. Ask first for the specification or test evidence with the highest effect on the content decision.

## Research rule

Verify time-sensitive or consequential claims with primary sources when possible. Record source and verification date. Distinguish discovery sources from proof. If access is blocked, label the claim unverified.
